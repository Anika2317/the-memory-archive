About

Alzheimer's changes memory. Not who someone is.

The forget-me-not has long stood for remembrance. The Memory Archive carries that meaning forward: a community campaign that preserves stories, challenges misconceptions about Alzheimer's, and connects generations through memory.

Alzheimer's can affect memory, but it does not erase a person's identity, relationships, or history. Like the petals of a single flower, each shared memory, whether a song, a recipe, or a place, is one part of a whole person. Together, the petals reveal the person behind the diagnosis.

Within the archive:

  • A network of memories, connected by the songs, places, and moments they share
  • A monthly question answered by families across San Diego
  • A collection organized by theme, decade, and year

Joyful, difficult, and unexpected moments are held alike, reflecting the full reality of living with Alzheimer's.

Every memory needs consent from the person or their family. We never publish names, addresses, or medical details. Every memory is reviewed before it appears, and families can ask us to remove theirs at any time.

Founded by Anika Seksaria and Maleesha Weerakkody, Del Norte High School, San Diego.
