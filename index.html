How it works

You don't need the whole story. Bring a piece.

1 · Collect

Fill the Mailbox

At our events and online, families drop in whatever they remember: a year, a place, a song, a recipe, a recording, or a drawing. The happy days, the hard days, and the surprising ones.

2 · Connect

It becomes a neuron

With permission, each memory becomes a cell in the archive. Every piece it shares with another memory forms a synapse between them.

3 · Remember

See the person

Open a memory to read it. Alzheimer's changes memory. It doesn't change who someone is.

About

Alzheimer's changes memory. Not who someone is.

[Your story: performing at ActivCare and local senior centers, your great-grandmother, and why you started The Memory Archive.]

Not every memory here is sunshine. Families also share the hard moments and the unexpected ones, like the first time a parent forgot a name, or the day a grandfather started painting. They sit right alongside the happy memories, because that is what living with Alzheimer's is really like, and talking about it openly is how stigma ends.

The Memory Archive is a community awareness campaign by Anika Seksaria and Maleesha [Last Name] at Del Norte High School. Every memory needs consent from the person or their family. We never publish names, addresses, or medical details. Every memory is reviewed before it appears, and families can ask us to remove theirs at any time.
